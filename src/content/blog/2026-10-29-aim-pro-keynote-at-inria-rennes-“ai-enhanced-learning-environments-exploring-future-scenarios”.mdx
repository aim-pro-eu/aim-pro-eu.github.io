The AIM-PRO project meeting in Rennes opened with an invited keynote at the **Inria centre at the University of Rennes**. On 20 October 2026, **Antonio Bucchiarone (University of L’Aquila)**, Scientific Coordinator of AIM-PRO, and **Jo Tondeur (Vrije Universiteit Brussel)** presented *“AI-Enhanced Learning Environments: Exploring Future Scenarios”* to researchers and students of the centre.

The keynote explored how artificial intelligence can be brought into learning environments in a way that supports, rather than replaces, the work of teachers and the growth of learners. Starting from current practices in schools, universities and professional training, the speakers outlined a set of future scenarios — from AI-supported lesson design and feedback to the vision of a Digital Twin of the learning path that follows teacher and student over time and offers each of them targeted guidance.

A distinctive feature of the talk was its **two-voice format**. The educational perspective, developed with **Sarah Howard (University of Leeds)** and **Thibaut Cromphaut,** met the computer science and software engineering perspective brought by Antonio Bucchiarone and his colleagues. This reflects a conviction at the core of AIM-PRO: bringing AI into education requires both the understanding of how people learn and the ability to design the systems that support them, and neither discipline can do it alone.

The keynote took place during the second AIM-PRO project meeting, hosted by the Université de Rennes on 20–21 October, and offered the consortium an opportunity to share its vision with the broader research community in Rennes. The partners thank **Benoit Combemale** and **Quentin Perez** for the invitation and for the warm hospitality.
